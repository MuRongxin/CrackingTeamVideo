take limited discovery in this action prior to the Rule 26(f) conference. Denuvo seeks an order

(“Defendant”). This motion is filed concurrently with the Declaration of Ryan C. Morris in 
Support of Expedited Discovery (“Morris Decl.”).

I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Defendant is a computer hacker, or a group of hackers, who has circumvented Denuvo’s 
technological measures that control access to copyrighted works, including video games, in 
violation of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (“DMCA”). Defendant goes by the online alias 
“voices38” and has not only circumvented Denuvo’s technological measures, but has disclosed 
online, including through such sites as Reddit, how to circumvent Denuvo’s technological controls 
for accessing copyrighted works. Because Defendant uses an alias online, Denuvo seeks limited 
discovery to learn Defendant’s identity so that Denuvo can serve Defendant.  
II. 
STATEMENT OF FACTS 
Denuvo is a world leader in video game security. Compl. ¶ 2. Among other things, Denuvo 
develops software programs for video game publishers that provide gaming security, preventing 
unauthorized access and piracy, and exposing cheats in competitive multiplayer games. Id.; id. 
¶¶ 10, 12. One of Denuvo’s security solutions is Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper product, which protects 
video games by reinforcing platform-level digital rights management (“DRM”). Id. ¶ 13. DRM 
manages legal access to digital content, restricting the use of copyrighted works by governing the 
use, modification, and distribution of copyright works. Id. ¶ 14. Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper ensures that 
only legitimate users can access a video game and prevents reverse engineering, debugging, and 
unauthorized modifications of the video game. Id. ¶¶ 15-16. Denuvo has provided its Anti-Tamper 
product to numerous video game publishers to enable those publishers to protect their copyrighted 
works. Id. ¶ 17.  
Defendant is a computer hacker who seeks to bypass or remove DRM for video games, 
including Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper, thereby circumventing the security measures developers and 
publishers use to prevent unauthorized access, copying, and distribution of video games. Compl. 
¶¶ 18, 20. Defendant has bypassed Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper for numerous copyrighted video games, 
removing the very protections that allow copyright owners to restrict who may access their works, 
and allowing for pirated versions of those copyrighted works to be used and distributed without 
authorization. Id. ¶¶ 23-49. After bypassing Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper for a video game, Defendant 
posted materials and instructions on the internet thereby allowing others to obtain pirated and

Denuvo has sought to determine the identity and location of Defendant. Denuvo has learned

III. 
ARGUMENT 
The Ninth Circuit has instructed that “‘where the identity of the alleged defendant[][is] not 
[] known prior to the filing of a complaint[,] the plaintiff should be given an opportunity through 
discovery to identify the unknown defendants, unless it is clear that discovery would not uncover 
the identities, or that the complaint would be dismissed on other grounds.’” Wakefield v. Thompson, 
177 F.3d 1160, 1163 (9th Cir. 1999) (quoting Gillespie v. Civiletti, 629 F.2d 637, 642 (9th Cir. 
1980)) (alteration in original). Other courts have reached the same conclusion. Murphy v. Goord, 
445 F.Supp.2d 261, 266 (W.D.N.Y. 2006); see also SunLust Pictures, LLC v. Doe, 2012 WL 
975828, at *2 (D. Colo. Mar. 22, 2012). This Court evaluates the following factors when considering 
motions for expedited discovery to identify an anonymous defendant: (1) whether the plaintiff has 
identified “the missing party with sufficient specificity such that the Court can determine that 
defendant is a real personal or entity who could be sued in federal court”; (2) the previous steps the 
plaintiff has taken to locate the defendant; and (3) whether the plaintiff’s suit could withstand a 
motion to dismiss. Columbia Ins. Co. v. seescandy.com, 185 F.R.D. 573, 578-80 (N.D. Cal. 1999).

These factors lead to a single conclusion: the Court should grant Denuvo’s request for expedited, 
limited discovery.  
First, Denuvo has identified Defendant with sufficient specificity to determine that 
Defendant is one or more real persons or entities that can be sued in federal court. Denuvo has 
identified Defendant as a person or persons operating online as “voices38.” Denuvo has determined 
Defendant’s username and User ID on an online voice, video, and text chat platform. Compl. ¶ 4; 
Morris Decl. ¶ 5. Denuvo has also determined that Defendant is associated with several accounts on 
a digital distribution platform owned and operated by Valve Corporation. Compl. ¶ 5; Morris Decl. 
¶ 6. Moreover, Defendant uses the online alias “voices38” on internet platforms such as Reddit. 
Compl. ¶¶ 3, 64. Indeed, Reddit pages are dedicated to Defendant’s hacking of video games, listing 
video games that Defendant has hacked, and providing materials and instructions for gaining 
unauthorized access to video games. Id. ¶ 64. There is sufficient information that Defendant is a 
person or combination of persons or entities that can be sued. 
Second, Denuvo has taken numerous steps to locate and identify Defendant. For instance, 
Denuvo found Defendant’s username and User ID on Discord, which is an internet platform for 
voice, video, and text chatting. Compl. ¶ 4; Morris Decl. ¶ 5. Denuvo has also learned of several 
accounts or profiles associated with Defendant on the digital distribution platform operated by 
Valve. Compl. ¶ 5; Morris Decl. ¶ 6. Further, Denuvo has identified certain transactions associated 
with Defendant, including transactions for cryptocurrencies. Morris Decl. ¶ 7. Denuvo has directly 
sought information about Defendant from companies such as Discord, Valve Corp., and Reddit, 
which emphasized that their privacy policies forbad them from disclosing information without 
compulsory legal process. Morris Decl. ¶ 8.  
Finally, Denuvo’s Complaint could withstand a motion to dismiss. Federal Rule of Civil 
Procedure 8(a) requires only “a short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is 
entitled to relief.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 8. This is a “liberal standard,” Fednav Ltd. v. Sterling Int’l, 572 
F.Supp.1268, 1270 (N.D. Cal. 1983), and the requirement of a short and plain statement is designed 
“to ‘give the defendant fair notice of what the … claim is and the grounds upon which it rests.’” 
Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 555 (2007). On a motion to dismiss under Rule

12(b)(6), the court accepts “all factual allegations in the complaint as true and construes the 
pleadings in the light most favorable to the nonmoving party.” Knievel v. ESPN, 393 F.3d 1068, 
1072 (9th Cir. 2005). “To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must contain sufficient factual 
matter, accepted as true, to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.” Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 
U.S. 662, 678 (2009). “A claim has facial plausibility when the plaintiff pleads factual content that 
allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct 
alleged.” Id. Detailed factual allegations are not required, id., and Denuvo’s Complaint sets forth 
substantial facts and details supporting its claims.  
Denuvo asserts claims under the DMCA for circumventing technological measures that 
control access to copyrighted works. Denuvo alleges that Defendant has violated Section 1201(a)(1), 
which provides that “[n]o person shall circumvent a technological measure that effectively controls 
access to a work protected under this title.” 17 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(1). Under the statute, “a 
technological measure ‘effectively controls access to a work’ if the measure, in the ordinary course 
of its operation, requires the application of information, or a process or a treatment, with the 
authority of the copyright owner, to gain access to the work.” Id. § 1201(a)(3)(B). And “to 
‘circumvent a technological measure’ means to descramble a scrambled work, to decrypt an 
encrypted work, or otherwise to avoid, bypass, remove, deactivate, or impair a technological 
measure, without the authority of the copyright owner.” Id. § 1201(a)(3)(A).  
Denuvo alleges that its Anti-Tamper protects copyrighted video games by reinforcing 
platform-level DRM, which governs the use, modification, and distribution of copyrighted works 
and of systems that enforce the policies within devices. Compl. ¶¶ 13-14. As such, Denuvo’s Anti-
Tamper is a process that ensures only legitimate users can play a video game, thereby controlling 
access to the copyrighted works. Id. ¶ 15. It also prevents reverse engineering, debugging, and 
unauthorized modifications of video games. Id. ¶ 16. Denuvo has provided the Anti-Tamper product 
to numerous video game publishers, including Sega, Ubisoft, Konami, Electronic Arts, Capcom, 
Game Science, Sony, 2K Games, Warner Bros. Games, and IO Interactive, which have added Anti-
Tamper to their copyrighted works. Id. ¶¶ 17, 24-62. Denuvo further alleges that Defendant is a 
computer hacker who is focused on reverse engineering and hacking video games employing

Denuvo also asserts that Defendant violated Section 1201(a)(2), which states that “[n]o

Moreover, this is not a situation in which “it is clear that discovery would not uncover the 
identit[y]’” of Defendant. Wakefield, 177 F.3d at 1163 (quoting Gillespie v. Civiletti, 629 F.2d 637, 
642 (9th Cir. 1980)). Denuvo has identified specific User IDs and accounts associated with 
Defendant on several platforms. See Compl. ¶¶ 4-5, 64; Morris Decl. ¶¶ 5-7. These platforms likely 
require registration by the user, including disclosure of certain contact information. Issuing 
subpoenas to these platforms will likely lead to information about Defendant that Denuvo can use 
to contact or further uncover information about Defendant. Attached to the Morris Declaration are 
the subpoenas that Denuvo seeks to issue. See Exs. A-C to Morris Decl. As can be seen, those 
subpoenas are designed to obtain information about Defendant’s identity and address to facilitate

IV. 
DENUVO SEEKS LEAVE TO DETERMINE DEFENDANT’S IDENTITY. 
Denuvo seeks leave of the Court to serve limited discovery on Discord, Inc., Valve 
Corporation, and Reddit, Inc. solely to determine Defendant’s true identity. Discord is an online 
platform for voice, video, and text chat. Valve Corporation owns the digital distribution platform 
Steam. And Reddit is a website platform for users to share user-generated content of various types. 
Denuvo has determined that Defendant has used each of these services and has accounts with them. 
Denuvo further understands that registrants for these platforms must provide certain identifying 
information in order to obtain an account. These services may also have additional information that 
would lead to Defendant’s identity. Denuvo has sought from each of these entities, and each has 
indicated that their privacy policies forbid disclosure of information without compulsory legal 
process, such as a duly issued subpoena. Morris Decl. ¶ 9. Accordingly, Denuvo seeks leave of the 
Court to serve subpoenas on these companies in furtherance of determining Defendant’s identity.  
V. 
CONCLUSION  
Denuvo has sought to identify Defendant without availing itself of the Court’s assistance 
but has been unsuccessful. For the foregoing reasons, Denuvo respectfully requests the Court grant 
leave to take expedited discovery prior to the Rule 26(f) conference to identify Defendant.
