Zavie
After letting my thoughts percolate a bit during the trip home from the party, I feel like I really need to write something, so I'll try...
Over the years, I've worked on, I guess you could call them cracks, from
console jailbreaks
to silly nonsense (e.g. cracking games I bought so I can run them on a new computer as I have long lost access to the email associated with my Steam acct). And, at times, it's been a very isolating experience.
Working for weeks and months on difficult hardware hacking stuff without having any progress to show for, while being barely able to talk to anyone about it. Cooperation being strained due to people wanting to cash out via bug bounties vs people wanting to make sure jailbreaks & homebrew are always viable for end users. And the stress from feeling like I'm the only person who both cares about the disastrous effect of "age-gating" laws, while also having the technical know-how to subvert the remote attestation implementations behind it. So many 'previous-generation' jailbreaking people end up working for the companies whose productsthey once attacked, while being at most worried about the privacy implications of what they are making, but not the control it gives to these companies.
Despite it all, this demo makes me feel like my efforts are not futile, that I am not alone, and that there will be others to pick up the slack once it's time for me to retire.
Thank you.

zeebr
I'm fed up with nostalgia pandering in PC demos.
Which makes it even more impressive that I love this demo so much. But it's doing so many things right that others don't.
First, the premise just fits. A 40 years group anniversary is _the_ occasion to pull on those Xennial heartstrings - and it comes from a genuine place. It's not some cheap faux-school attempt, it's an honest reminiscence, and that alone elevates it miles above the usual unprompted boing ball you see so often.
And then all the details are just right. Pretty much all the typography is proper PETSCII/ASCII/ANSI, with the right fonts, the right palettes, the right techniques, the right UI and beloved tools from each platform, and a visible love for the medium and the culture around it. And when it deviates from "canon", it's done with artistic intent. This alone is enough to raise the bar for this kind of demo for hopefully the next decades to come. Right? ... right? O.O
But then it goes on. That soundtrack. The way it switches from SID to Amiga to AdLib to modern production, each one again done just right, and then mixes it all up and goes extra hard, and all of this while staying true to its melancholic nature with those lyrics and those 80s and Futurepop vibes. The way it's doing all that and still gets to be an instant earworm.
And as if that's not enough already, all of it is wrapped in (almost) impeccable direction. It keeps throwing thing after thing at you, but all of it makes sense narratively, and the motion graphics and transitions, as detailed and overwhelming as they sometimes seem, act as a guide through the eras. You may lose track of everything that's happening right now, but the emotional and story beats always hit.
My one point of criticism would be the last "act" where it goes newschool. You can feel the visuals running out of steam a bit. It's still good but the effects and transitions lack some of the polish and momentum that the previous acts had, and that fact clashes with the music going into full-on mode at the same time.
But that's a pretty minor nitpick in the grand scheme of things. The whole production just oozes love and dedication, and has an emotional quality that only very few demos are able to reach.
And also all those fake endings and including your PC's desktop in the right places - *chef's kiss*.
