 1 I.       INTRODUCTION

 2          Defendant is a computer hacker, or a group of hackers, who has circumvented Denuvo’s

 3 technological measures that control access to copyrighted works, including video games, in

 4 violation of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (“DMCA”). Defendant goes by the online alias

 5 “voices38” and has not only circumvented Denuvo’s technological measures, but has disclosed

 6 online, including through such sites as Reddit, how to circumvent Denuvo’s technological controls

 7 for accessing copyrighted works. Because Defendant uses an alias online, Denuvo seeks limited

 8 discovery to learn Defendant’s identity so that Denuvo can serve Defendant.

 9 II.      STATEMENT OF FACTS

10          Denuvo is a world leader in video game security. Compl. ¶ 2. Among other things, Denuvo

11 develops software programs for video game publishers that provide gaming security, preventing

12 unauthorized access and piracy, and exposing cheats in competitive multiplayer games. Id.; id.

13 ¶¶ 10, 12. One of Denuvo’s security solutions is Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper product, which protects

14 video games by reinforcing platform-level digital rights management (“DRM”). Id. ¶ 13. DRM

15 manages legal access to digital content, restricting the use of copyrighted works by governing the

16 use, modification, and distribution of copyright works. Id. ¶ 14. Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper ensures that

17 only legitimate users can access a video game and prevents reverse engineering, debugging, and

18 unauthorized modifications of the video game. Id. ¶¶ 15-16. Denuvo has provided its Anti-Tamper

19 product to numerous video game publishers to enable those publishers to protect their copyrighted

20 works. Id. ¶ 17.

21          Defendant is a computer hacker who seeks to bypass or remove DRM for video games,

22 including Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper, thereby circumventing the security measures developers and

23 publishers use to prevent unauthorized access, copying, and distribution of video games. Compl.

24 ¶¶ 18, 20. Defendant has bypassed Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper for numerous copyrighted video games,

25 removing the very protections that allow copyright owners to restrict who may access their works,

26 and allowing for pirated versions of those copyrighted works to be used and distributed without

27 authorization. Id. ¶¶ 23-49. After bypassing Denuvo’s Anti-Tamper for a video game, Defendant

28 posted materials and instructions on the internet thereby allowing others to obtain pirated and

15 III.     ARGUMENT

16          The Ninth Circuit has instructed that “‘where the identity of the alleged defendant[][is] not

17 [] known prior to the filing of a complaint[,] the plaintiff should be given an opportunity through

18 discovery to identify the unknown defendants, unless it is clear that discovery would not uncover

19 the identities, or that the complaint would be dismissed on other grounds.’” Wakefield v. Thompson,

20 177 F.3d 1160, 1163 (9th Cir. 1999) (quoting Gillespie v. Civiletti, 629 F.2d 637, 642 (9th Cir.

21 1980)) (alteration in original). Other courts have reached the same conclusion. Murphy v. Goord,

22 445 F.Supp.2d 261, 266 (W.D.N.Y. 2006); see also SunLust Pictures, LLC v. Doe, 2012 WL

23 975828, at *2 (D. Colo. Mar. 22, 2012). This Court evaluates the following factors when considering

24 motions for expedited discovery to identify an anonymous defendant: (1) whether the plaintiff has

25 identified “the missing party with sufficient specificity such that the Court can determine that

26 defendant is a real personal or entity who could be sued in federal court”; (2) the previous steps the

27 plaintiff has taken to locate the defendant; and (3) whether the plaintiff’s suit could withstand a

28 motion to dismiss. Columbia Ins. Co. v. seescandy.com, 185 F.R.D. 573, 578-80 (N.D. Cal. 1999).

14          Second, Denuvo has taken numerous steps to locate and identify Defendant. For instance,

23          Finally, Denuvo’s Complaint could withstand a motion to dismiss. Federal Rule of Civil

24 Procedure 8(a) requires only “a short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is

25 entitled to relief.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 8. This is a “liberal standard,” Fednav Ltd. v. Sterling Int’l, 572

26 F.Supp.1268, 1270 (N.D. Cal. 1983), and the requirement of a short and plain statement is designed

27 “to ‘give the defendant fair notice of what the … claim is and the grounds upon which it rests.’”

28 Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 555 (2007). On a motion to dismiss under Rule

 1 12(b)(6), the court accepts “all factual allegations in the complaint as true and construes the

 2 pleadings in the light most favorable to the nonmoving party.” Knievel v. ESPN, 393 F.3d 1068,

 3 1072 (9th Cir. 2005). “To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must contain sufficient factual

 4 matter, accepted as true, to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.” Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556

 5 U.S. 662, 678 (2009). “A claim has facial plausibility when the plaintiff pleads factual content that

 6 allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the misconduct

 7 alleged.” Id. Detailed factual allegations are not required, id., and Denuvo’s Complaint sets forth

 8 substantial facts and details supporting its claims.

 9          Denuvo asserts claims under the DMCA for circumventing technological measures that

10 control access to copyrighted works. Denuvo alleges that Defendant has violated Section 1201(a)(1),

11 which provides that “[n]o person shall circumvent a technological measure that effectively controls

12 access to a work protected under this title.” 17 U.S.C. § 1201(a)(1). Under the statute, “a

13 technological measure ‘effectively controls access to a work’ if the measure, in the ordinary course

14 of its operation, requires the application of information, or a process or a treatment, with the

15 authority of the copyright owner, to gain access to the work.” Id. § 1201(a)(3)(B). And “to

16 ‘circumvent a technological measure’ means to descramble a scrambled work, to decrypt an

17 encrypted work, or otherwise to avoid, bypass, remove, deactivate, or impair a technological

18 measure, without the authority of the copyright owner.” Id. § 1201(a)(3)(A).

19          Denuvo alleges that its Anti-Tamper protects copyrighted video games by reinforcing

20 platform-level DRM, which governs the use, modification, and distribution of copyrighted works

21 and of systems that enforce the policies within devices. Compl. ¶¶ 13-14. As such, Denuvo’s Anti-

22 Tamper is a process that ensures only legitimate users can play a video game, thereby controlling

23 access to the copyrighted works. Id. ¶ 15. It also prevents reverse engineering, debugging, and

24 unauthorized modifications of video games. Id. ¶ 16. Denuvo has provided the Anti-Tamper product

25 to numerous video game publishers, including Sega, Ubisoft, Konami, Electronic Arts, Capcom,

26 Game Science, Sony, 2K Games, Warner Bros. Games, and IO Interactive, which have added Anti-

27 Tamper to their copyrighted works. Id. ¶¶ 17, 24-62. Denuvo further alleges that Defendant is a

28 computer hacker who is focused on reverse engineering and hacking video games employing

 1          Moreover, this is not a situation in which “it is clear that discovery would not uncover the

 2 identit[y]’” of Defendant. Wakefield, 177 F.3d at 1163 (quoting Gillespie v. Civiletti, 629 F.2d 637,

11 IV.      DENUVO SEEKS LEAVE TO DETERMINE DEFENDANT’S IDENTITY.

12          Denuvo seeks leave of the Court to serve limited discovery on Discord, Inc., Valve

13 Corporation, and Reddit, Inc. solely to determine Defendant’s true identity. Discord is an online

14 platform for voice, video, and text chat. Valve Corporation owns the digital distribution platform

15 Steam. And Reddit is a website platform for users to share user-generated content of various types.

16 Denuvo has determined that Defendant has used each of these services and has accounts with them.

17 Denuvo further understands that registrants for these platforms must provide certain identifying

18 information in order to obtain an account. These services may also have additional information that

19 would lead to Defendant’s identity. Denuvo has sought from each of these entities, and each has

20 indicated that their privacy policies forbid disclosure of information without compulsory legal

21 process, such as a duly issued subpoena. Morris Decl. ¶ 9. Accordingly, Denuvo seeks leave of the

22 Court to serve subpoenas on these companies in furtherance of determining Defendant’s identity.

23 V.       CONCLUSION

24          Denuvo has sought to identify Defendant without availing itself of the Court’s assistance

25 but has been unsuccessful. For the foregoing reasons, Denuvo respectfully requests the Court grant

26 leave to take expedited discovery prior to the Rule 26(f) conference to identify Defendant.
